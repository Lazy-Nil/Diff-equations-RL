ODE Diagnostic Matrix, Exact Differentials & Picard Flowchart

Recognizing these 9 canonical differential combinations allows you to bypass standard integration and rewrite non-linear terms directly as exact total differentials $du$:

01. Product Rule
$$x\,dy + y\,dx = d(xy)$$
02. Radial Sum
$$x\,dx + y\,dy = d\left(\frac{1}{2}(x^2 + y^2)\right)$$
03. Quotient (y/x)
$$\frac{x\,dy - y\,dx}{x^2} = d\left(\frac{y}{x}\right)$$
04. Quotient (x/y)
$$\frac{y\,dx - x\,dy}{y^2} = d\left(\frac{x}{y}\right)$$
05. Log Quotient
$$\frac{x\,dy - y\,dx}{xy} = d\left(\log\frac{y}{x}\right)$$
06. Polar Angle (y/x)
$$\frac{x\,dy - y\,dx}{x^2 + y^2} = d\left(\tan^{-1}\frac{y}{x}\right)$$
07. Polar Angle (x/y)
$$\frac{y\,dx - x\,dy}{x^2 + y^2} = d\left(\tan^{-1}\frac{x}{y}\right)$$
08. Log Product
$$\frac{x\,dy + y\,dx}{xy} = d\big(\log(xy)\big)$$
09. Arcsine Product
$$\frac{x\,dy + y\,dx}{\sqrt{1 - x^2 y^2}} = d\big(\sin^{-1}(xy)\big)$$

When $\frac{\partial M}{\partial y} \neq \frac{\partial N}{\partial x}$, use this diagnostic matrix to select the exact Integrating Factor $\mu(x,y)$:

RULE I & II (STRUCTURAL)
Homogeneous ($Mx+Ny \neq 0$): $$\mu = \frac{1}{Mx + Ny}$$ Product Form $f(xy)y\,dx + F(xy)x\,dy = 0$: $$\mu = \frac{1}{Mx - Ny}$$
RULE III & IV (SINGLE-VARIABLE)
Function of $x$ alone: $$\frac{1}{N}\left(\frac{\partial M}{\partial y} - \frac{\partial N}{\partial x}\right) = f(x) \implies \mu = e^{\int f(x)dx}$$ Function of $y$ alone: $$\frac{1}{M}\left(\frac{\partial N}{\partial x} - \frac{\partial M}{\partial y}\right) = \phi(y) \implies \mu = e^{\int \phi(y)dy}$$
RULE V (MONOMIAL $\mu = x^a y^b$)
For polynomial ODEs where Rules I–IV fail, multiply by $\mu = x^a y^b$, equate $\frac{\partial(\mu M)}{\partial y} = \frac{\partial(\mu N)}{\partial x}$, and solve the 2×2 linear system for exponents $(a, b)$.

STEP 1: CONTINUITY OF f(x,y)

Is $f(x,y)$ continuous and bounded ($|f(x,y)| \le M$) on $R: |x-x_0|\le a, |y-y_0|\le b$? If YES, at least one solution Exists.

STEP 2: LIPSCHITZ CONDITION

Is $\left|\frac{\partial f}{\partial y}\right| \le L$ continuous and bounded on $R$? If YES, the solution is Unique on $|x-x_0| \le h = \min(a, b/M)$.

STEP 3: CLASSICAL BENCHMARKS

• $y' = 2\sqrt{y},\; y(0)=1 \implies$ Unique $y=(x+1)^2$
• $y' = 2\sqrt{y},\; y(0)=0 \implies$ Infinite ($\partial f/\partial y$ blows up)
• $y' = 1/\sqrt{y},\; y(1)=0 \implies$ Unique (Thm fails, sol exists)
